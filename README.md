# Temporal Patterns in Benthic Communities on the Seaward Wall at Durban Harbour (2010–2014)

## Project overview
A five-year ecological research project examining temporal and spatial variation in benthic community composition on the seaward-facing wall at the entrance to Durban Harbour, KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa.

The study analysed **655 photo-quadrats** collected between 2010 and 2014. It investigated changes in percentage cover, biodiversity and community composition across sampling years, depth zones and sections of the wall.

## Analytical scope and methods
The wider project examined:
- Percentage cover of dominant benthic categories.
- Taxon richness, Shannon diversity and Pielou's evenness.
- Differences among years, depths and wall sections.
- Community composition using fourth-root transformed cover, Bray–Curtis similarity and non-metric multidimensional scaling (NMDS).

Methods reported in the project included:
- Data preparation and descriptive summaries in Microsoft Excel (individual contribution).
- Kruskal–Wallis tests with false-discovery-rate adjustment.
- Two-way ANOVA to examine depth, section and depth-by-section effects.
- PERMANOVA, PERMDISP and SIMPER.
- NMDS ordination.

The report identifies JMP and PRIMER-E (with PERMANOVA+) as statistical software used in the wider project. Do not list these as personal software skills unless you personally used them.

## Selected findings
- Algal turf had the highest overall mean cover (**61.49%**) and occurred in **94.0%** of quadrats.
- Mean algal turf cover declined from **71.72% in 2010** to **35.59% in 2014**.
- Mean taxon richness increased from **2.50 taxa per quadrat in 2010** to **4.18 in 2014**.
- Mean Shannon diversity increased from **0.468** to **1.064** over the same period.
- Community composition differed among years, depths and sections in the PERMANOVA results; the report also notes important limitations involving unequal sampling and differences in multivariate dispersion.
